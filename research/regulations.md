# Regulations: septic tanks and treatment plants (England)

Researched 24 November 2026. These rules apply to **England only**. All site copy about rules must match this file. If a rule changes, update this file first, then the pages.

Sources (all read in full, November 2026):
- GOV.UK, General binding rules: small sewage discharge to surface water (last updated 2 October 2023): https://www.gov.uk/guidance/general-binding-rules-small-sewage-discharge-to-a-surface-water
- GOV.UK, General binding rules: small sewage discharge to the ground (last updated 23 July 2026): https://www.gov.uk/guidance/general-binding-rules-small-sewage-discharge-to-the-ground
- GOV.UK, Septic tanks and sewage treatment plants: what you need to do: https://www.gov.uk/permits-you-need-for-septic-tanks (and its subpages: existing discharges, new discharges, find out if you need a permit)

## 1. The core rule that drives the business

**A septic tank must not discharge directly to a watercourse** (stream, river, ditch). Where one does, the owner must deal with it "as soon as possible". GOV.UK gives three options:
1. Connect to the public foul sewer.
2. Replace the septic tank with a small sewage treatment plant.
3. Install a drainage field so the septic tank discharges to ground under the ground rules.

GOV.UK: "You must have plans in place to do this work within a reasonable timescale, usually 12 months."

The old deadline was **1 January 2020** (or sooner if the property was sold before then). It has passed, so any septic tank still going to a watercourse is non-compliant now.

**Selling a property.** GOV.UK says buyer and seller should agree who will replace or upgrade the system, as a condition of sale. It does not say the seller must pay; that is a matter for the two parties. Do not write "the seller must replace it". Write "the buyer and seller need to agree who will do it, as a condition of sale".

Retrofit units fitted inside a septic tank only count for surface water discharges if installed before 2007. Any reedbed or secondary treatment unit after a septic tank must meet the relevant British Standard, or the owner must upgrade or apply for a permit.

## 2. Volume limits (general binding rules)

| Discharge to | Limit |
|---|---|
| Surface water (river, stream) | 5 m³ (5,000 litres) a day |
| Ground (drainage field) | 2 m³ (2,000 litres) a day |

Above the limit: connect to the sewer if reasonable, otherwise apply for a permit. Residential flow is worked out with the Defra daily discharge calculator; commercial and holiday lets use British Water's *Flows and Loads* guidance.

## 3. Standards

- Treatment plants: **BS EN 12566** (BS EN 12566-3 is the part for packaged treatment plants; the ground rules say BS EN 12566 for septic tanks and treatment plants).
- Drainage fields: **BS 6297:2007**.
- Evidence of compliance: CE/UKCA mark, certificate of compliance in the paperwork, or listing on British Water's approved equipment list.
- Systems installed before 1983 do not have to meet a standard but must meet the other rules.
- New systems (and any installed after 1 February 2006) must be sized to British Water *Flows and Loads*.
- Approved Document H 1.55: packaged treatment works should be type-tested (BS 7781 or by a notified body). 1.56: if it needs power, it should cope without power for up to 6 hours or have an uninterruptible supply.

## 4. Discharges to ground

- Must go through a **drainage field** (or a drainage mound built to BS 6297:2007, not in a floodplain).
- **Not allowed:** a well, borehole, or a rainwater-type soakaway installed after December 2007. Shafts and concrete rings should be avoided. A direct discharge to groundwater means no permit and the system must be replaced.
- Engineered ground (from 23 July 2026): allowed if not in a floodplain, a minimum percolation value (Vp) of 15, and built to BS 6297:2007.
- Copy note: people search "soakaway" when they mean drainage field. Use the word in headings for SEO but explain the difference honestly.

## 6. Sewer distance and when you can't use the general binding rules (discharges started on or after 1 January 2015)

- Single home: cannot meet the rules if a public foul sewer is **within 30 m of any boundary** of the property.
- Developments: 30 m multiplied by the number of homes.
- Protected sites, surface water: in or within 500 m of an SSSI, SPA, SAC, Ramsar site, bathing water, shellfish water or freshwater pearl mussel population; within 200 m of an aquatic local nature reserve; within 50 m of a chalk river or aquatic local wildlife site.
- Protected sites, ground: in ancient woodland, or in or within 50 m of an SAC, SPA, Ramsar site or SSSI.
- Groundwater Source Protection Zone 1 (not SPZ1c): permit needed. Anywhere within 50 m of a private drinking water supply counts as SPZ1.
- Surface water discharge must go to water that flows all year (not a dry ditch, not an enclosed pond).
- New surface water discharges need a permit if within 50 m of another general binding rules system.
- Check protected sites on Defra's Magic map.

## 7. Existing vs new discharges

- **Existing:** started before 2 October 2023 and not changed type, not moved more than 10 m, not increased above the volume limit.
- **New:** started, changed type, moved more than 10 m, or increased on or after 2 October 2023.
- Replacing a septic tank with a treatment plant may make it a new discharge. Check every job.

## 8. Building regulations and planning

- **Building regulations approval is required** to install a new septic tank or small sewage treatment plant. Planning permission may also be needed.
- If the current system never had building regulations approval, the owner may need to apply.
- Somerset Council building control fee for this work: not found online. **Rich to update** after calling building control.

## 9. Selling: what the seller must hand over (both rule sets)

"If you sell your property, you must tell the new operator ... in writing that a sewage discharge is in place." The note must include:
- a description of the treatment system and drainage system
- where the main parts and discharge point are
- details of any changes made
- how it should be maintained, plus the manual if there is one
- maintenance records, if they exist

## 10. Maintenance

- Maintain to the manufacturer's instructions.
- Desludge "before it goes beyond the maximum capacity", and "at least once a year or in line with the manufacturer's instructions".
- The sludge must be taken by a **registered waste carrier**.
- Maintenance should be done by someone competent, for example a British Water accredited service engineer.
- Repair or replace if there are leaks, cracks, blockages, poor drainage, smells, or a failed motor, pump or electrics.
- When a system stops being used for good, remove anything that could pollute (e.g. leftover sludge).

## 11. Permits

- You only need a permit if you cannot meet the general binding rules and connecting to the sewer is not reasonable.
- "You will not be given a permit if you could meet the general binding rules."
- Operating without a permit when one is needed is breaking the law.

## 12. Somerset phosphates (local angle)

The Somerset Levels and Moors is a Ramsar site. Since 2020 new housing in its catchment has had to be "nutrient neutral" for phosphorus. Upgrading old septic tanks and treatment plants can create **phosphate credits** that developers buy.

- Somerset Council runs its own P-credit scheme (renamed from the River Tone scheme in June 2025; covers Tone and Parrett catchments). Contact: phosphates@somerset.gov.uk. https://www.somerset.gov.uk/planning-buildings-and-land/somerset-council-phosphate-credit-scheme/
- Somerset Council lists third-party credit providers: https://somerset.gov.uk/planning-buildings-and-land/potential-third-party-phosphate-mitigation-schemes-in-the-somerset-levels-and-moors-catchment-area
- WCI Group (Somerset) offers free septic tank upgrades to eligible homeowners in the Parrett, Tone, Brue and Axe catchments in return for the credits. WCI says the Parrett catchment covers south Somerset, which includes Yeovil.
- Natural England / EA advice note: replacing some systems that discharge to ground cannot create credits. Eligibility is case by case.
- National policy is changing (Environmental Delivery Plans, water company upgrades by 2030). Check before writing about this.

**Copy rule:** never promise a free upgrade. Say some homeowners may qualify for funded upgrades, and that we will tell them honestly if a scheme may suit them better than paying us.

## Registration
England no longer requires septic tanks or treatment plants to be registered with the Environment Agency if they meet the general binding rules. (The old exemption registration ended when the general binding rules began on 1 January 2015.) Wales and Scotland still have registration schemes.

## Other UK nations
Wales (Natural Resources Wales) and Scotland (SEPA) have different rules. The site covers England only, and should say so.
