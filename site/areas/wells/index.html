Septic tanks and off-mains drainage around Wells

Wells is our home patch. England's smallest city sits right under the Mendip Hills, and the springs that gave it its name still flow through the Bishop's Palace gardens. The city centre is on the mains, but homes on the Mendip slopes around Wookey Hole, Easton, Priddy and Westbury-sub-Mendip are often on limestone with fast, fissured drainage and protected groundwater. Head south towards Coxley and the moors and you get the opposite problem: low, wet ground where a drainage field struggles.

We work across Wells and nearby villages including Wookey, Wookey Hole, Easton, Coxley, Dinder, Westbury-sub-Mendip.

Local rules: River Sheppey and the catchment

Wells is on the River Sheppey, which we understand drains to the Somerset Levels and Moors, an internationally protected wetland (Ramsar site). That matters in two ways:

  • New homes in the catchment have to be "nutrient neutral" for phosphorus, which can affect the drainage design for self-builds and conversions.
  • Existing homes may be able to get an upgrade funded through a phosphate credit scheme. How the Somerset schemes work.

Catchment lines don't follow town boundaries, so check your exact property with Somerset Council.

Building regulations approval for a new treatment plant or septic tank goes through Somerset Council building control or a registered building control approver. We prepare and submit the application as part of the job.

Common questions

Do I need to replace my septic tank when selling a house in Wells?

If it discharges to a stream, river or ditch, it needs upgrading, and the buyer and seller should agree who will do the work as a condition of sale. If it goes to a working drainage field, it may be fine. A survey will tell you.

Is Wells in the Somerset phosphate area?

We understand Wells drains to the Somerset Levels and Moors, so phosphate rules and credit schemes may be relevant. Check your exact property with Somerset Council.
