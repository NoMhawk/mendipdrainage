Septic tanks and off-mains drainage around Dorchester

Dorchester and the chalk valleys around it drain to the Dorset Frome and on to Poole Harbour, which has its own nutrient rules for new development. Chalk can drain very fast, so percolation results here can be too quick rather than too slow, and some areas are groundwater Source Protection Zones.

We work across Dorchester and nearby villages including Charminster, Puddletown, Cerne Abbas, Maiden Newton, Piddlehinton, Broadmayne.

Local rules: River Frome and the catchment

Dorchester is on the River Frome, which flows to Poole Harbour. New development in the Poole Harbour catchment is subject to nutrient rules, which can affect drainage design for new homes and conversions. Check your property with Dorset Council. The Somerset phosphate schemes do not apply here.

Building regulations approval for a new treatment plant or septic tank goes through Dorset Council building control or a registered building control approver. We prepare and submit the application as part of the job.

Common questions

Do I need to replace my septic tank when selling a house in Dorchester?

If it discharges to a stream, river or ditch, it needs upgrading, and the buyer and seller should agree who will do the work as a condition of sale. If it goes to a working drainage field, it may be fine. A survey will tell you.

Is Dorchester in the Somerset phosphate area?

We understand Dorchester is outside the Somerset Levels and Moors catchment, so the Somerset schemes are unlikely to apply. Other nutrient rules may apply to new development here.
