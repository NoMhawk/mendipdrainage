Why old septic tanks are being replaced

Under the Environment Agency's general binding rules, a septic tank in England must not discharge to a stream, river or ditch. Thousands of older rural homes still do, often without the owner knowing.

It usually comes to light when a house is sold: the buyer's solicitor asks where the waste water goes. If the answer is "the ditch", it needs upgrading, usually to a modern sewage treatment plant.

Never seen inside a septic tank? Here's how it all works

Most people have never had to think about where their toilet goes. These two diagrams explain the difference in about 30 seconds.

Septic tank

Separates the solids. The soil does the cleaning.

  • No electricity needed
  • No moving parts to break
  • Cheaper to buy
  • Fine if your ground drains well
  • Must not go to a stream, river or ditch
  • Needs a drainage field and enough garden
  • Struggles on heavy clay or wet ground
  • Many old ones are cracked or too small

Sewage treatment plant

Cleans the water itself, with air and natural bacteria.

  • Much cleaner water out
  • Can go to a stream that flows all year
  • Works where the ground drains badly
  • Often needs less garden than a large drainage field
  • Most need electricity for an air pump
  • Needs a yearly service
  • Costs more to buy

Common questions

Do I need to replace my septic tank?

If it discharges to a stream, river or ditch, yes: under the general binding rules a septic tank must not discharge to a watercourse. If it goes to a working drainage field and is in good order, it may be fine. A survey will tell you.

How much does it cost to replace a septic tank?

For a normal house, a full replacement with a treatment plant typically costs £7,000 to £15,000. We give a fixed written quote after a free site visit.

I don't know anything about septic tanks. Is that a problem?

Not at all. Most people with a septic tank have never had to think about where their drains go. We explain everything in plain English, show you where everything is, and give you a simple guide to looking after it.

How much mess will it make?

There will be a digger and some open ground for a few days. We plan the route to keep disruption down, protect drives and paths where we can, and put the ground back when we finish. Your quote says exactly what reinstatement is included.

Which areas do you cover?

Wells and everywhere within about an hour: the Mendips, central and south Somerset, Bath and the Chew Valley, Weston-super-Mare and Burnham, and the edges of Dorset and Wiltshire.

I am selling my house. Can you help quickly?

Yes. We can survey the system, give you a written report for the buyer and a fixed quote if work is needed. Tell us your completion date when you call.
