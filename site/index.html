Why old septic tanks are being replaced

Under the Environment Agency's general binding rules, a septic tank in England must not discharge to a stream, river or ditch. Thousands of older rural homes still do. The rule bites hardest when a house is sold, because the buyer's solicitor asks where the waste water goes. If the answer is "the ditch", it needs upgrading, usually to a modern sewage treatment plant.

We are a Yeovil-based team that does one thing well: replacing old septic tanks with systems that meet today's rules, properly designed, installed and signed off. Read the rules in plain English.

Common questions

Do I need to replace my septic tank?

If it discharges to a stream, river or ditch, yes: under the general binding rules a septic tank must not discharge to a watercourse. If it goes to a working drainage field and is in good order, it may be fine. A survey will tell you.

How much does it cost to replace a septic tank?

For a normal house, a full replacement with a treatment plant typically costs £7,000 to £15,000. We give a fixed written quote after a free site visit.

Which areas do you cover?

I am selling my house. Can you help quickly?

Yes. We can survey the system, give you a written report for the buyer and a fixed quote if work is needed. Tell us your completion date when you call.
