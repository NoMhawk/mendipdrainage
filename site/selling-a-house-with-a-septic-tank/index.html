Selling a house with a septic tank

A septic tank should not stop a sale. But a non-compliant one, or one with no paperwork, can slow it down or knock the price. Here is what the rules say and how we help sellers, buyers and agents.

What the rules say about selling

Under the general binding rules, when you sell a property with a septic tank or treatment plant you must tell the buyer in writing that a sewage discharge is in place. The written note must include:

  • a description of the treatment system and drainage system
  • where the main parts and the discharge point are
  • details of any changes made to the system
  • how it should be maintained, and the maintenance manual if you have one
  • maintenance records, if you have them

If the septic tank discharges to a watercourse, it has to be replaced or upgraded. GOV.UK says the buyer and seller should agree who will be responsible for the work, as a condition of sale.

What the buyer and their solicitor will ask

  • Is it a septic tank, a treatment plant or a cesspit?
  • Where does it discharge: a drainage field, a soakaway, or a stream or ditch?
  • Is it shared with neighbours? Is there a written agreement?
  • Does it have building regulations approval?
  • When was it last emptied and serviced?
  • Is it inside your boundary? If not, are there rights to access and maintain it?

Mortgage lenders may also want answers. Having them ready avoids weeks of back and forth.

Common questions

Who pays to replace a septic tank when a house is sold?

There is no fixed rule. GOV.UK says the buyer and seller should agree who is responsible, as a condition of sale. Often the seller pays or the price is reduced by the cost of the work.

Can I sell a house with a non-compliant septic tank?

Yes, but the buyer must be told and the upgrade has to be agreed as part of the sale. Expect the buyer to ask for a quote and a price reduction, or for the work to be done before completion.

Is there a septic tank compliance certificate?

There is no single official certificate. What buyers look for is evidence: building regulations approval, product certification (such as BS EN 12566), service and emptying records, and the written description the rules require. A survey report brings this together.

How long does it take?

A survey can usually be booked within days. Replacement takes 3 to 5 days on site once building control is in place. Tell us your completion date and we will say honestly whether it can be done in time.
