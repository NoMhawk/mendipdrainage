Treatment plant servicing

A treatment plant is a small machine working every day. A yearly service keeps it running, catches faults early, and gives you the records buyers and the Environment Agency expect.

Service
from £150
How often
At least yearly, or as the maker says
Emptying
typically £200 to £350, by a registered waste carrier
Records
Written service report each visit

What the rules say

The general binding rules say you must maintain your system as the manufacturer says, have the sludge removed before it goes beyond the maximum capacity (at least once a year, or as the manufacturer says), and use a registered waste carrier to take it away. The person doing the maintenance should be competent, for example a British Water accredited service engineer.

You must also repair or replace the system if it is not in good working order, for example leaks, cracks, blockages, smells or a failed motor, pump or electrics.

What a service includes

  • Check the air pump or motor, alarms and controls
  • Check diffusers, filters and baffles
  • Measure the sludge level and arrange emptying if needed
  • Look at the effluent and the outfall or drainage field
  • Written report with any faults and recommended fixes

Common questions

Do you service plants you did not install?

Yes, most common makes. Tell us the make and model when you book.

My plant alarm is going off. What should I do?

Do not ignore it. It usually means a pump, air blower or high level problem. Cut back water use and call us or your service company.
