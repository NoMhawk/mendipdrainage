Converting a septic tank to a treatment plant

Conversion units fit inside an existing septic tank and turn it into a treatment plant. They suit some sites. But the rules on watercourse discharges are strict, so check before you buy one.

Good for
Sound tanks discharging to ground
Watch out
Watercourse discharges
Alternative
Full replacement with a new plant
First step
A survey of the existing tank

What is a conversion unit?

A conversion (or retrofit) unit is a kit that sits inside an old septic tank and adds air and extra treatment, turning it into something close to a treatment plant. It means less digging than a full replacement.

The rule you need to know

The general binding rules for discharges to surface water say a retrofit system inside an existing septic tank is only acceptable if it was installed before 2007. Any reedbed or secondary treatment unit fitted after a septic tank must meet the relevant British Standard. If it doesn't, you have to upgrade to a treatment plant or apply for a permit.

In practice, if your tank discharges to a stream or ditch, a new conversion unit is unlikely to be the answer unless it is a certified system that meets the standard. Check with the maker and the Environment Agency before you pay for one.

When conversion can make sense

  • The tank is structurally sound, watertight and the right size.
  • The unit is certified to the relevant British Standard for the use you need.
  • Access is very tight and replacing the tank would be very costly.

When full replacement is better

  • The tank is old brick or concrete, cracked, or leaking.
  • It discharges to a watercourse.
  • It is undersized for the house.
  • You are selling and want a clear, certified system on paper.

We will look at your tank and tell you honestly which route is right. See septic tank replacement.

Common questions

Is a conversion unit cheaper than a new plant?

Often the unit is cheaper and there is less digging. But if the tank is in poor condition or the unit does not meet the rules for your discharge, it can cost more in the end.

Will a buyer accept a converted tank?

Buyers and their solicitors want proof the system meets the rules. Certified equipment, building control records and a service history help. Without them, expect questions.
