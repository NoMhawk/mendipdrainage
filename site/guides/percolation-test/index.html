Percolation tests explained

A percolation test measures how fast water soaks into your soil. It decides whether a drainage field can work and how big it needs to be.

How the test is done

Approved Document H sets out the method:

  1. Dig a trial hole at least 2m deep (or 1.5m below the planned pipes) to check the water table.
  2. Dig a 300mm square test hole, 300mm below where the pipes will go.
  3. Fill it with water to at least 300mm and let it soak away overnight.
  4. Next day, refill it and time how long the water takes to drop from 75% to 25% full (150mm).
  5. Divide the time in seconds by 150. That is the Vp, seconds per millimetre.
  6. Repeat at least three times in at least two holes, and take the average.

Don't test in heavy rain, frost or drought.

What the result means

  • Vp between 12 and 100: a drainage field can work.
  • Below 12: water drains too fast to clean the effluent properly.
  • Above 100: the ground drains too slowly, common in heavy clay.

The winter water table must also stay at least 1m below the pipes. If the ground fails, a treatment plant to a flowing watercourse may be the answer.
