How often should a septic tank or treatment plant be emptied?

The short answer from the rules: before it gets too full, and at least once a year, or as the manufacturer says.

What the rules say

The general binding rules say you must get the sludge removed before it goes beyond the maximum capacity of the system, and you should do this at least once a year or in line with the manufacturer's instructions. The company taking it away must be a registered waste carrier. Ask to see their certificate.

What affects it

  • Number of people in the house
  • Size of tank or plant
  • Type of plant: some makers give longer intervals
  • What goes down the drain: wipes, fats and chemicals make things worse

Septic tank or treatment plant: any difference?

The rule is the same for both: empty before it is too full, at least yearly or as the maker says. In practice, a treatment plant with too much sludge can carry solids through to the outlet and stop treating properly. A septic tank left too long pushes solids out into the drainage field, which can block it for good. A blocked drainage field costs far more to fix than a yearly empty.

Booking an empty

  • Check the company is a registered waste carrier and ask for a copy of the certificate.
  • Make sure the tanker can reach. Approved Document H expects a septic tank to be within 30m of vehicle access.
  • For a treatment plant, check whether the maker says to empty fully or partly, and whether to refill it with water afterwards.
  • Keep the receipt. Emptying records help when you sell.

Cost

Typically £200 to £350 per empty, depending on size and access. A treatment plant also needs a yearly service by a competent engineer, for example a British Water accredited service engineer. We install, we do not service, but the plant maker can recommend someone local.
