Buying a house with a septic tank

Lots of lovely rural homes around Wells and the Mendips are off the mains. A septic tank is nothing to fear, but you should know what you are buying before you exchange.

Questions to ask the seller

  • Is it a septic tank, treatment plant or cesspit?
  • Where does it discharge?
  • Is it shared? Is there a written agreement for costs and access?
  • Is it all within the boundary? If not, what rights are there?
  • When was it last emptied and serviced? Are there records?
  • Does it have building regulations approval?

The seller must give you a written description of the system under the general binding rules.

Get it surveyed

A normal building survey will not usually tell you if it meets the rules. A specialist septic tank survey will. If it needs replacing, a written quote gives you a real number to negotiate with.

If it needs upgrading

GOV.UK says buyer and seller should agree who will do the work, as a condition of sale. That might mean the seller does it before completion, or you agree a lower price and do it after. Typical cost for a full replacement is £7,000 to £15,000. See prices.

Running costs to budget for

  • Emptying: about £200–£350 a time, usually yearly
  • Treatment plant service: from £150 a year
  • Electricity for a treatment plant

Common questions

Will I get a mortgage on a house with a septic tank?

Usually yes. Lenders may ask questions about a non-compliant system, so have the answers ready.
