Septic tank vs sewage treatment plant

They look similar from the garden, but they work differently and the rules treat them differently. The main question is where the water goes.

How each one works

Septic tank: a tank with two or more chambers. Solids sink, scum floats, and the liquid flows out. No power, no moving parts. The liquid is still dirty, so it must be cleaned by the soil in a drainage field.

Treatment plant: settles the solids too, but then adds air so bacteria break down the waste, and settles it again. Needs electricity. The water leaving it is much cleaner.

Septic tank

Separates the solids. The soil does the cleaning.

  • No electricity needed
  • No moving parts to break
  • Cheaper to buy
  • Fine if your ground drains well
  • Must not go to a stream, river or ditch
  • Needs a drainage field and enough garden
  • Struggles on heavy clay or wet ground
  • Many old ones are cracked or too small

Sewage treatment plant

Cleans the water itself, with air and natural bacteria.

  • Much cleaner water out
  • Can go to a stream that flows all year
  • Works where the ground drains badly
  • Often needs less garden than a large drainage field
  • Most need electricity for an air pump
  • Needs a yearly service
  • Costs more to buy

Which do you need?

  • Only a stream or ditch to discharge to, or clay soil? A treatment plant, discharging to a watercourse that flows all year.
  • Free-draining ground and space for a field? A septic tank with a drainage field can work and has no running costs. A treatment plant to a drainage field is also an option.
  • Public sewer nearby? Connecting may be the right answer.

Common questions

Is a treatment plant better than a septic tank?

It cleans the water better and gives you more choice about where it goes. But it costs more to buy and run. If your ground drains well, a septic tank and drainage field is still a good, legal option.
