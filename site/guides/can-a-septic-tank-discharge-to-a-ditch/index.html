Can a septic tank discharge into a ditch or stream?

No. In England, a septic tank must not discharge directly to a watercourse. That includes streams, rivers and ditches. If yours does, it needs to be upgraded.

What the rules say

The general binding rules for surface water discharges only allow a small sewage treatment plant to discharge to a watercourse. Where a septic tank does, GOV.UK says you must replace or upgrade it as soon as possible, with plans in place within a reasonable time, usually 12 months.

What about a treatment plant to a ditch?

Even a treatment plant can only discharge to surface water that flows throughout the year. A ditch that dries up in summer does not count (except in drought). An enclosed pond or lake is not allowed either.

How do I know where mine goes?

Many owners don't know. Clues: a pipe coming out into a ditch or stream bank near the tank, no wet patch or field in the garden, or old deeds mentioning an outfall. A septic tank survey will find it.

Your options

  1. Connect to the mains sewer, if reasonable.
  2. Replace the tank with a sewage treatment plant, which can use the same outfall if the watercourse flows all year.
  3. Keep the tank and add a drainage field, if the ground drains.

Common questions

My septic tank has gone to the stream for 50 years. Does it still need changing?

Yes. Age does not make it compliant. The old deadline of 1 January 2020 has passed.
