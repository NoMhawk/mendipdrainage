Why does my septic tank smell?

A faint smell near the tank lids can be normal. A strong, lasting smell in the garden or house is a warning sign.

Common causes

  • The tank is full and needs emptying.
  • Blocked or missing vent on the tank or the house soil pipe.
  • Failed drainage field, letting effluent come to the surface.
  • Cracked tank or loose lids.
  • Treatment plant fault: a failed air blower lets the plant go septic and smell.
  • Dried-out traps inside the house.

Smell in the house or the garden?

Inside the house, the cause is often closer to home: a dry trap on a little-used sink or shower, a failed toilet seal, or a blocked vent stack. Run water into every outlet and check the vent pipe on the roof is clear.

In the garden, a brief smell near the tank lids after use can be normal. A smell over the drainage field, or one that lasts all day, usually means the tank is full, the drainage field is failing, or effluent is reaching the surface.

At the stream or ditch, a smell or grey water where the pipe comes out means poorly treated effluent is getting out. If that is a septic tank, it should not be discharging there at all under the general binding rules.

What to do

Start with emptying and checking the lids and vents. If a treatment plant smells, check the blower is running and the alarm is not on. If the smell keeps coming back, book a survey. The rules say you must fix a system that causes smells.
