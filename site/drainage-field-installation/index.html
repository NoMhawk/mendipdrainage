Drainage field installation

A drainage field spreads the liquid from your septic tank or treatment plant into the soil, where it is cleaned naturally. Many people call it a soakaway. Done properly, it is designed from a percolation test and built to BS 6297.

Typical cost
£2,500 to £6,000 on its own
Standard
BS 6297:2007 and Approved Document H
Test first
Percolation test and trial hole
Distance
At least 15m from any building

Drainage field or soakaway?

People often say "soakaway" for any hole that takes waste water away. For sewage, the rules are clear: a septic tank must discharge to ground through a drainage field built to BS 6297:2007. A rainwater-style soakaway installed after December 2007 is not allowed for sewage. Read drainage field vs soakaway.

How we design one

  1. Trial hole. At least 2m deep, to find the winter water table. It must not come within 1m of the pipes.
  2. Percolation test. We time how fast water soaks away, at least three times in at least two holes, as Approved Document H sets out. The result (Vp) needs to be between 12 and 100.
  3. Sizing. The area of the field is worked out from the Vp result and the number of people.
  4. Siting. At least 10m from any watercourse, 15m from any building, 50m from any water supply borehole or spring, and away from driveways and services.

More on the test: percolation tests explained.

Signs your drainage field has failed

  • Wet, boggy or very green grass over the field
  • Sewage smells in the garden
  • The septic tank fills up quickly or drains back up
  • Water pooling near the tank outlet

If your ground does not drain

Heavy clay is common in parts of Somerset. If your percolation test fails, a drainage field may not work. Options include a treatment plant discharging to a flowing watercourse, or a drainage mound. We will tell you what the test shows and what it means.

Common questions

How big does a drainage field need to be?

It depends on the soil and the number of people. Approved Document H gives a formula based on the percolation result. A typical house field is tens of square metres of trenches.

Can I drive over a drainage field?

No. Approved Document H says no access roads, driveways or paved areas should be in the disposal area.
