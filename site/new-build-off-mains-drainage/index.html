Can you reasonably connect to the sewer?

Sewer first

For new discharges, the general binding rules do not apply if a public foul sewer is within 30m of the property boundary (or 30m times the number of homes for a development). Natural England and the Environment Agency also say new development should connect to the sewer where reasonable. We check this first.

Phosphates and new homes in Somerset

Much of Somerset, including Wells, drains to the Somerset Levels and Moors Ramsar site. New homes in that catchment need to show they will not add phosphorus (nutrient neutrality). That can affect your choice of treatment system and whether you need mitigation. See phosphate credits in Somerset, and talk to the council planning team early.

Holiday lets and commercial use

Holiday lets, B&Bs and businesses use British Water's Flows and Loads figures, which can be higher than for a normal house. Above 2,000 litres a day to ground, or 5,000 litres a day to a watercourse, the general binding rules no longer apply: you need to connect to the public sewer if that is reasonable, or apply for a permit.

Common questions

Do I need a permit for a new treatment plant?

Not if you meet the general binding rules. You need a permit if you cannot meet them, for example because of volume, protected sites or a groundwater Source Protection Zone 1, and connecting to the sewer is not reasonable.
