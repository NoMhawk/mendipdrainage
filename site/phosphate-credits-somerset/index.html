Septic tanks and phosphate credits in Somerset

Somerset's phosphate problem has created schemes that pay to upgrade old septic tanks. Some homeowners may get an upgrade funded. Here is how it works, in plain English, and how to find out if it applies to you.

Why phosphates matter here

The Somerset Levels and Moors is a Ramsar site, an internationally important wetland. Too much phosphorus in the rivers that feed it harms wildlife. Since 2020, new housing in the catchment has had to be "nutrient neutral", meaning it must not add phosphorus overall.

Where septic tanks come in

Old septic tanks and poor treatment plants release phosphorus. Upgrading them reduces it. Natural England and the Environment Agency have advised that upgrading some existing septic tanks and treatment plants can create "phosphate credits", which developers can then use to get planning permission for new homes.

Somerset Council runs its own phosphate credit scheme, and lists approved third-party providers. At least one local firm offers free upgrades to eligible homeowners in the Parrett, Tone, Brue and Axe catchments, in return for the credits.

Does it apply to me?

Maybe. Eligibility depends on where your property is, what system you have, and where it discharges. Natural England's advice says some systems, for example certain ones that already discharge to ground, cannot create credits.

Common questions

Is a free septic tank upgrade really free?

Under the schemes we have seen, the provider pays for the upgrade because they sell the phosphate credits it creates. Read the terms carefully, including who owns and maintains the new plant and any legal agreement on your property.

Does this apply in Dorset?

Parts of north Dorset, such as around Sherborne, drain to the Somerset Levels and Moors. Other parts of Dorset are covered by different nutrient rules, such as Poole Harbour. Check with Dorset Council.
