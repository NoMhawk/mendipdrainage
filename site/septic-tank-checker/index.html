Is your septic tank compliant?

Answer four quick questions for a plain-English answer. Nothing is sent anywhere.

1. What do you have?
2. Where does the liquid go?
3. Any problems?
4. Are you buying or selling soon?

Please answer all four questions.

What the checker looks at

The check is based on the Environment Agency's general binding rules for small sewage discharges in England. The big questions are:

  • What kind of system you have. Septic tanks and treatment plants are treated differently.
  • Where the liquid goes. A septic tank must go to ground through a drainage field. A treatment plant can go to a stream that flows all year.
  • Whether it is working. The rules say a system that isn't in good working order must be repaired or replaced.
  • Whether you're selling. Sellers must give buyers a written description of the system.

It can't check everything. A nearby public sewer, a protected wildlife site, or a private drinking water supply can all change the answer. A septic tank survey gives you a definite answer in writing.

Don't know where yours goes?

That's very common. Clues to look for:

  • A pipe coming out into a ditch or stream bank near the tank
  • A patch of extra-green or soggy grass in the garden (often a drainage field or soakaway)
  • Old deeds or sale paperwork mentioning an "outfall"
  • A small electric box or kiosk near the lids (likely a treatment plant)
