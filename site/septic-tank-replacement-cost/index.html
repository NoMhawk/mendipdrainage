Septic tank replacement cost

For a normal three or four bedroom house, replacing a septic tank with a sewage treatment plant typically costs £7,000 to £15,000, all in. Here is what is included and what moves the price.

Full replacement (typical house)
£7,000 to £15,000
Treatment plant unit only
about £2,000 to £5,000
New drainage field
£2,500 to £6,000
Survey
from £295

What's in a typical replacement price

  • Site survey and design, including plant sizing to British Water Flows and Loads
  • The treatment plant (BS EN 12566-3)
  • Emptying and removing or filling the old tank
  • Digging, bedding, backfill and pipe connections
  • Power supply and controls by a registered electrician
  • Building regulations application
  • Commissioning, maintenance notice and handover pack
  • Basic reinstatement of the ground

What pushes the price up or down

FactorLower costHigher cost
Where the treated water goesExisting outfall to a stream that flows all yearNew drainage field or a pumped outfall
Plant size2 to 3 bedrooms5+ bedrooms, annexes, holiday lets
GroundEasy digging, low water tableRock, high water table, unstable ground
AccessOpen access for a digger and a tankerNarrow access, steep slopes, long pipe runs
Old tankFilled in placeFull removal required
ReinstatementRough lawnDrives, patios, landscaped gardens

Running costs to budget for

  • Emptying: typically £200 to £350, at least yearly or as the maker says
  • Yearly service: from £150
  • Electricity for the plant's air pump or motor

Where our figures come from

Our ranges are based on published UK trade cost guides and online plant prices, checked in November 2026. They are a guide, not a quote. You get a fixed written price after a free site visit.

Common questions

Why don't other firms publish prices?

Every site is different, so a fixed price needs a visit. But ranges help you plan, so we publish ours and then give a fixed written quote after a free site visit.

Is VAT included?

Our ranges include VAT. Your written quote will show it clearly.

Are there grants?

There is no general national grant. In parts of Somerset, phosphate credit schemes may fund upgrades for some homeowners. See our phosphate credits page.
